MANIFESTO & METHODOLOGY

ABOUT VNFOLKS

“Exploring the space between folklore and knowledge.”

“VNFOLKS began with a question: what happens when ancient stories meet modern curiosity?

Across Vietnam, generations have passed down stories about spirits, mysterious creatures, dreams, omens, rituals, and the night sky. Some were created to explain the unknown. Some carried warnings. Others became part of cultural memory.

This project does not exist to prove or disprove belief.

Instead, VNFOLKS explores the space between folklore and knowledge — asking where these stories came from, what they meant to the people who told them, and what science can explain today.”
SECTION 01

WHY VNFOLKS EXISTS

VNFOLKS was created as a personal digital archive dedicated to documenting, contextualizing, and exploring Vietnamese folklore and traditional beliefs through an empathetic, interdisciplinary lens. Too often, ancient folk narratives are either dismissed as baseless superstition or stripped of their rich cultural roots in commercial horror tropes.

This archive treats folklore as an invaluable record of human adaptation. When our ancestors encountered dark banyan groves, treacherous river whirlpools, terrifying sleep paralysis, or the sudden midday darkness of a solar eclipse, they lacked modern polysomnography, hydrology, and orbital mechanics. Yet they were not passive. They created vivid symbolic stories, moral warnings, and communal rituals to preserve life, comfort grief, and make meaning of the cosmos.

SECTION 02

CULTURE & SCIENCE

The guiding philosophy of VNFOLKS is that culture and science are not opposing enemies, but two distinct ways humanity has sought to understand the world. We firmly reject the simplistic premise that “ancient people were wrong and science is right.”

When we examine Bóng Đè alongside REM sleep atonia and hypnagogic hallucination neurobiology, science explains the mechanism of the experience, while folklore explains its emotional and cultural meaning. When we study the warning myth of the water specter Ma Da, hydrology and fluid dynamics explain the fatal underwater undertow, while folklore explains how a rural riverine society taught children water safety without written warning signs. By examining both lenses side-by-side, we gain a deeper reverence for both human heritage and scientific progress.

SECTION 03

THE STORIES WE INHERIT

Every culture inherits stories from the dark. In Vietnam, these legends were shaped by centuries of monsoon rains, lush subtropical jungles, labyrinthine delta waterways, and the profound historical resilience of a wet-rice farming civilization.

Whether it is the cautionary figure of Ông Ba Bị carrying three woven sacks through rural lanes, the haunting annual feast for forgotten souls during the Seventh Lunar Month, or the heroic sacrifice of Thần Độc Cước standing on coastal cliffs, these narratives remain part of Vietnam’s living identity. VNFOLKS invites modern readers—scholars, students, and curious minds worldwide—to discover these timeless tales with respectful curiosity.
