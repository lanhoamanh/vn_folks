ABOUT THIS PROJECT

ABOUT VNFOLKS

“Exploring the space between folklore and knowledge.”

“VNFOLKS began with a simple question: what happens when ancient stories meet modern curiosity?

Across Vietnam, families have passed down stories about spirits, mysterious creatures, dreams, omens, and the night sky. Some were created to explain the unknown, some carried safety warnings for kids, and others became family traditions.

This website doesn't try to prove or disprove anyone's beliefs.

Instead, it explores the connection between folklore and real-life knowledge—asking where these stories came from, what they meant to the people telling them, and what modern science can teach us about them today.”
PART 01

WHY I MADE VNFOLKS

I grew up hearing so many fascinating Vietnamese folk stories from my parents and grandparents, but when I searched for them online in English, there was barely anything besides generic horror tropes.

I wanted to create a site where anyone could learn about these tales with context and respect. When ancient villagers saw eclipses or woke up unable to move from sleep paralysis, they didn't have telescopes or modern sleep clinics. They explained what they experienced through vivid, memorable stories that brought communities together.

PART 02

STORIES & SCIENCE

A big goal of this project was showing that folklore and science don't have to be opposites.

For example, when we look at Bóng Đè alongside sleep paralysis and REM sleep biology, science explains how the body freezes up, while folklore shows how people felt and the rituals they used to calm themselves. Looking at both sides helps us appreciate how observant our ancestors really were.

PART 03

STORIES FROM HOME

Every culture has bedtime stories and spooky warnings. In Vietnam, these legends grew out of centuries of village life along misty rivers, thick bamboo groves, and rice paddies.

Whether it's the cautionary warning about Ông Ba Bị to keep kids from wandering near dangerous water, the spooky youth ritual of Ma Lon, or the folklore around river spirits like Ma Da, these tales are a meaningful piece of Vietnamese culture. I hope this project makes them fun and easy to explore!
